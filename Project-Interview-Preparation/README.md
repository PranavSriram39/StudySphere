## 🗺️ Learning Path & Recommended Order

To gain a solid, compounding understanding of the project, study the chapters in this strict sequence:

1. **Chapter 1 & 2:** Master the "Why". Understand the business value, the domain (EdTech/Collaboration), and how to pitch the project. *(Non-technical / High-level)*
2. **Chapter 3:** Master the "How at a macro level". Understand the System Architecture and Data flow. *(System Design)*
3. **Chapter 4, 5, & 6:** Master the "How at a micro level". Deep dive into Backend, Frontend, and specific lines of code. *(Core Technical)*
4. **Chapter 7 & 8:** Master the "Senior Engineer perspective". Learn about Advanced Engineering, Scalability, Deployment, and Security. *(Advanced / DevOps)*
5. **Chapter 9 & 10:** Master the "Interview Environment". Practice the Q&A, behavioural questions, and project the vision of a Principal Engineer. *(Execution & Future)*

## 🎯 Interview Preparation Strategy

* **The "T-Shaped" Approach:** Go broad on the architecture (Chapter 3) and go deep on the specific modules you built or are most proud of (e.g., AI Quiz Generation, WebRTC).
* **The STAR Method:** For behavioral and project questions, always frame your answers using **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, and **R**esult.
* **Tradeoff Articulation:** Never say a technology is "the best". Always say, "We chose X over Y because of Z, but the tradeoff is W." This shows maturity.
* **Whiteboarding:** Practice drawing the diagrams from Chapter 3 on a physical whiteboard or a tool like Excalidraw while talking out loud.

## ❌ Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Memorizing without understanding:** Interviewers will ask follow-up "Why?" questions. If you only memorize, you will freeze.
2. **Ignoring the business value:** Don't just talk about WebRTC and Llama-3.3. Talk about *why* the user needs it (solving remote education gaps).
3. **Over-complicating early answers:** Start with a simple 30-second summary before diving into deep technical jargon.
4. **Hiding limitations:** Be honest about what the system *cannot* do yet (e.g., it might struggle with 10,000 concurrent WebRTC users on a single node). Acknowledging limitations is a senior trait.
